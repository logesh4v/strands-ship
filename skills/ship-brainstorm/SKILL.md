# Recipe 1: brainstorm your idea

There is **no default project**. Every team brings its own people and problem; identical agents from one community cost every team originality points. The examples in `~/.strands-ship/kit/examples/ideas.json` (HeatSafe Shift, Padippu, Marundhu Clear, FloodReady) show what a strong entry looks like; they are not a menu to copy. `references/council.md` shows how two frontier models judged them, a method you can reuse.

## Step 1: start from people, not technology (ask in one message)
1. Who do you (or your college, family, NSS unit, an NGO) actually reach, and could you talk to five of them this week? (students in a government school, farmers in your village, outdoor workers, elderly patients, a flood-prone ward...)
2. What goes wrong for them that costs time, money, health or safety? Ask for one real story.
3. Which focus area does it sit in: education, health or climate resilience?
4. What could you count this week, before and after? (minutes saved, wrong answers caught, people reached, unsafe advice avoided)
5. Languages they use, and what devices (feature phone, shared smartphone, a teacher's laptop)?
6. Who else in your college or user group is entering, and what are they building?

## Step 2: three ideas, honestly scored
From their answers, write three distinct ideas (they may be in one focus area). For each: one-line pitch; who it helps; the measurable improvement and how to measure it in a week; the agent's one decision that must never be wrong; the biggest risk (safety, data, feasibility). Score each 1-10 on the four rubric quarters (social impact, technical execution, communication, originality) plus "buildable in the days left", and show the numbers.

## Step 3: argue it out
Take the top idea and argue **against** it the way a Gate 2 judge would (is the impact measurable? is it just the hackathon page's own example? would a plain prompt do as well? who could be harmed?). Then steelman it: what would have to be true for it to win? Revise or switch if it does not survive. If two ideas tie, the one with people you can reach this week wins.

## Step 4: make it distinct
If another team in the community has a similar idea, change the user group, the district, the language or the decision, not just the name. Say plainly if the idea matches one of the examples in ideas.json or on the hackathon page; it can still win, but only with measured evidence the example lacks.

## Step 5: write IDEA.md
Path name, pitch, who, the story from Step 1, the decision that must never be wrong, what will be measured, languages, the scores and the argument from Step 3, and "not this week" items. Then: say "design my agent".
